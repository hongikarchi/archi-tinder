archibe Business Model PRD

status strategy draft date 2026-06-04 KST scope student data, office fit, portfolio logs format static HTML
Executive Thesis

archibe should not become another architecture job board. It should become a taste-based office discovery and talent intelligence layer for Korean architecture students, junior designers, and small to mid-size offices.

User promise

Find offices that fit my taste.

Students use swipes, saved references, boards, and project logs to build a low-friction architecture identity before a polished portfolio exists.

Office promise

Find students who fit our work.

Offices see opt-in candidates whose taste, process, and project direction match the office's built work and hiring needs.

Business promise

Monetize fit, not raw job posts.

Job posting is a commodity. Fit reports, verified profile campaigns, student shortlist insight, and studio reports are more defensible.

Strategic Rule

RULEDo not sell organic ranking. Paid office products can improve profile quality, campaign targeting, analytics, and clearly labeled sponsored placement. They should not force low-fit offices into high-fit recommendation slots.

Business Model Ladder
StageOfferBuyerWhy buyer pays
0. Free supply Free office profile, free basic job post, free student taste profile. Students, offices. Build useful database density before monetization pressure.
1. Pilot sponsor Beta partner badge, profile polishing, fit-audience report, campaign exposure. 5 to 10 small/mid offices. Low-cost brand access to architecture students and junior talent.
2. Verified office Blue badge, editable profile, hiring CTA, analytics, rich office recommendation card. Hiring offices. Signals legitimacy and improves recruiting presentation.
3. Fit intelligence Opt-in candidate shortlist, student-office fit report, project-log match explanation. Offices hiring interns or juniors. Reduces review time and reveals candidates who match the office's design direction.
4. Reports and schools Aggregated trend reports, studio archive, portfolio workshop package. Schools, offices, industry sponsors. Turns anonymized student taste and project data into market insight.
Pricing Hypotheses
Early pilot

3 months, KRW 300k to 1M

Use as a paid learning contract, not a full SaaS subscription. Include profile setup, one campaign, and one insight report.

Verified office

Monthly or seasonal subscription

Start around a price that is materially higher than a job-board listing only if analytics and fit reports create visible value.

Studio package

Workshop or cohort pricing

Offer taste profiling, project archive, and exportable student profile pages for architecture studios or portfolio classes.

Ad revenue

Peripheral, not survival core

Display ads can offset small infrastructure cost on editorial pages, but app UX and recommendation trust are more valuable.

AdSense Position

AdSense can be added later to magazine-style, SEO-friendly pages if traffic becomes large. It should not be placed in the core swipe, profile, office recommendation, or recruiting flows.

  • AdSense RPM is calculated as estimated earnings divided by page views, multiplied by 1000.
  • Even optimistic niche traffic usually does not fund team survival at early scale.
  • Architecture recruiting trust is worth more than generic ad impressions.
Legal and Trust Boundary

Early BM should avoid success fees, direct placement fees, or anything framed as paid job placement. Keep the product framed as office discovery, recruiting brand tools, information service, and opt-in candidate insight.

  • Label sponsored office exposure clearly.
  • Never expose private student data without explicit opt-in.
  • Do not charge based on hiring success until legal registration and process are reviewed.
  • Keep organic fit score separate from paid campaign placement.
Student Data Graph
Taste graph

Swipes, likes, saves, boards

The existing recommendation loop creates a low-friction taste profile. This becomes the foundation for office recommendation and student-office fit.

Project graph

Project-unit portfolio logs

Students upload projects in smaller units instead of waiting for a finished portfolio. This captures process, growth, and real studio work.

Office Follow

Office follow should not behave like a daily social feed. Architecture offices do not publish new buildings every week. Follow should be treated as a career-intent signal.

Project Log MVP
Low burden

Post a project, not a full portfolio.

Each project can have a title, cover image, 3 to 8 images, short description, tags, school year, and visibility setting.

Process value

Show thinking, not only final renders.

Stages can include concept, reference, diagram, model, plan, section, render, critique, and final.

Recruiting value

Give offices better evidence.

Offices see how students define problems, iterate, handle feedback, and express spatial ideas.

Visibility and Safety
  • Private: visible only to the student.
  • Profile public: visible on the student's public archibe profile.
  • Recruiting only: visible to verified offices when the student opts into recruiting.
  • Studio archive: visible inside a school/studio cohort, if the school partnership exists.

Main Risks
Trust risk

Pay-to-rank perception

If students believe recommendations are bought, the core asset dies. Sponsored surfaces must be labeled and fit thresholds must remain real.

Supply risk

Low project-log upload rate

Students may avoid uploads if the flow feels like a finished portfolio. MVP must be lightweight and useful for profile/export.

Demand risk

Small offices may not pay

Do not assume willingness. Run paid pilots before building complex analytics or subscriptions.

Legal risk

Placement-fee boundary

Avoid success fees or direct placement framing until legal review and registration path are clear.

Internal Product Assumptions
  • Core promise: 10 to 15 swipes should capture subtle taste.
  • Primary business persona priority: firm to jobseeker before person-to-person or firm-to-client.
  • Out of scope: romance, real-estate transactions, contractor matching, materials marketplace, CAD marketplace, and design-service marketplace.
  • Korea-first market: English can be supported, but Korean UX and Korean architecture community density drive early adoption.
